Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript "Exact nonequilibrium steady state of collective
group-flip dynamics on quenched interaction neighborhoods" for consideration
as a Regular Article in Physical Review E.

Analytical results for group-update dynamics of two-state units (majority
rule, nonlinear and group-driven voter models) are almost always obtained on
annealed structures, where the interacting group is redrawn at every step,
and the systems relax to absorbing consensus. A recent example in this journal
is the group-driven voter model of Kim et al. [Phys. Rev. E 111, L052301
(2025)]. The manuscript addresses two questions that this line of work leaves
open: whether a group-update dynamics on a quenched structure can be solved
exactly, and whether an annealed description with the same group size
reproduces the macroscopic state.

For a group-flip rule, in which a firing node and every node sharing its state
inside its fixed metric neighborhood switch together, the main results are:

1. An exact stationary majority fraction, E[M] = (2-q)/(3-2q) with q the
   neighborhood coverage, valid for every finite system size and independent of
   dimension and topology, under two explicit geometric conditions and equal
   neighborhood sizes. The proof uses an exact affine closure of the mean
   minority size; no moment closure or large-n limit is involved. Closed forms
   follow for the mean absolute magnetization, the rotation and erasure
   currents and the cycle-length law, and for unequal neighborhoods the closure
   survives with one effective parameter and gives exact bounds.

2. A noise-free nonequilibrium steady state: there is no absorbing state, the
   majority fraction equals the fraction of updates that reverse the sign of
   the magnetization, and one-way erasure transitions break detailed balance at
   an exactly computed rate.

3. A quantitative separation between quenched and annealed neighborhoods: the
   annealed version with the same neighborhood size orders less at every size
   computed (up to n = 3200), and the exact lemmas identify the mechanism
   (correlation between compact minority domains and neighborhoods).

4. Exact finite-size solutions below the solvable regime, with a closed-form
   one-step extinction probability and a conjectured upper bound supported by
   all 17 ring cases with n <= 33.

All results are labelled by status (theorem, numerical observation,
heuristic, conjecture), and the limitations (two states, extensive
neighborhoods required by the majority condition, unproven conjecture below
the solvable regime) are stated in the abstract and the discussion. Complete
proofs, solver descriptions, validation records and an exploratory regression
test are in the Supplemental Material; code and data are openly available
(Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24050030).

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration
elsewhere. I believe it fits the scope of Physical Review E in nonequilibrium
statistical physics and stochastic dynamics on networks.
